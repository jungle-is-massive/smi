The framework

Not a benchmark.
A blueprint.

Most brands know they should be doing more with social. Very few know what "more" actually looks like — or where they're genuinely falling short.

The Social Maturity Index gives marketing leaders a structured, honest view of where their social ecosystem stands across five critical dimensions: content, creators, distribution, data, and governance.

It's not an audit of what you're posting. It's a diagnostic of how you're built to operate — and where the gaps are costing you.

What you get
  • A scored view of your brand across all five pillars
  • Your maturity stage: Emerging, Developing, Operational, or Strategic
  • A clear picture of where your gaps are and what they're costing you
  • Prescriptive recommendations mapped to your exact stage
  • A benchmark against where leading brands typically sit
  • A custom growth roadmap from a Jungle strategist

Where you'll land

Four stages.
One direction.

Every brand lands in one of four stages. There's no shame in Emerging — the point is knowing exactly where you are so you can move.

0–25 · Emerging
Fragmented & reactive.
Social is happening, but it's not a system. Output is driven by brief-to-brief thinking, creator choices are gut-driven, and there's no meaningful measurement in place.
What this looks like
  • Reactive posting — content follows events, not strategy
  • No consistent creator approach or ROI tracking
  • Paid and organic treated as separate channels
  • Reporting is output-focused, not insight-led
  • No formal briefing structure or review cycle
Jungle recommendation Start with a JungleIQ audit to uncover foundational gaps. Launch a pilot content system with measurable goals and native-first creative.
26–45 · Developing
Systems emerging. Consistency missing.
Some structure is in place. Content quality is improving. But creator partnerships, data systems, and governance are inconsistent — which caps what's possible.
What this looks like
  • A content calendar exists but isn't always followed
  • Creator work happens but isn't consistently measured
  • Some paid amplification, no clear framework for it
  • Basic reporting in place, not yet actionable
  • Some clarity on roles, but cross-team friction exists
Jungle recommendation Engage Pollinate for structured creator partnerships. Introduce modular content formats and a distribution framework via Growth Lab.
46–60 · Operational
Strong creative. Scale is the next step.
Good creative and real structure. The opportunity now is integration — connecting data across channels, scaling creator programmes, and formalising how the whole system talks to itself.
What this looks like
  • Content is platform-native and produced at volume
  • Creators selected on fit and tracked on ROI
  • Paid and organic inform each other, mostly
  • Real-time data access — but not always acted on
  • Clear roles, but system-level thinking is still emerging
Jungle recommendation Integrate JungleIQ dashboards across teams. Expand test-and-learn. Formalise cross-functional roles and ops to remove friction at scale.
61–75 · Strategic
Mature. Data-led. Room to innovate.
A genuinely mature content system. Data-led, modular, scalable. The focus shifts from building the system to pushing its edges — creator-led performance loops, commerce journeys, system innovation.
What this looks like
  • Modular content engine operating at scale
  • Creator network structured, measured, performing
  • Distribution fully integrated — paid feeds organic
  • Insight drives decisions, benchmarks are live
  • Governance is clear, reviewed quarterly, evolving
Jungle recommendation Use Growth Lab to unlock system innovation — retail commerce journeys, creator-led performance loops. Share learnings internally and externally.
Designed for

The brands ready to know.

The SMI is most useful when you're prepared to be honest about where you are. That takes a certain kind of marketing leader.

ICP 1 · Established brands
The tired brands.
Heavy ATL spenders with significant social underinvestment. Board-level recognition that something needs to change.
  • £5m+ ATL budgets with understated social investment
  • Retail, FMCG, homewares, telco, travel — big-box brands
  • Know they're behind on social but don't have a clear view of how far
  • Looking for a framework to make the internal case for change
  • DFS is the prototype: zero to owned social operating system
ICP 2 · Growth brands
The growth-hungry SMEs.
Mid-market brands (£10–15m revenue) that want a full-service partner and a system that scales with them.
  • Revenue £10–15m, 50–250 employees
  • No agency roster — want strategy, creative, influence, and paid in one place
  • Fast to convert, sticky once embedded
  • Want to build social as a genuine growth engine, not a support channel
  • Relationships that compound as the brand grows
Role · Marketing leadership
The people in the room.
The SMI is built for the leaders who own the social budget and the brief — not for their agencies.
  • CMOs making the case to their board for social investment
  • Heads of Social who know their system has gaps but can't articulate them
  • Brand Directors feeling the gap between ATL spend and social output
  • Marketing Directors preparing for an agency review
Trigger · The right moment
When to use it.
The SMI lands hardest at a specific moment in the brand's journey — usually when something has just changed.
  • New CMO or Head of Marketing in post — first 90 days
  • Brand relaunch or major campaign in planning
  • Agency review underway or just concluded
  • Declining social performance with no clear diagnosis
  • Competitor is visibly outperforming on social
